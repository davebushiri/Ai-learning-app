# Pitch

## One paragraph (for the submission form)

**Stop the Apprentice** trains the skill that keeps tradespeople safe: noticing when something is about to go wrong. Instead of quizzing the learner, we flip the roles. An AI apprentice talks through a real job out loud, like swapping an outlet, brazing a line set, or doing a brake job, and somewhere along the way makes mistakes. Some are sloppy and some could kill someone. The learner is the supervisor with one big STOP button. They hit it, say what's wrong in their own words, and Claude grades the catch like a journeyman would. Missed mistakes end in a short "here's what happened next." Scoring rewards early catches, penalizes false alarms, and treats a missed critical mistake as a failure. That mirrors how judgment actually gets built on a jobsite: by watching someone else's work and catching it in time. Every job is generated fresh, so a new trade is a new prompt, not new software. It runs in a browser with voice in and out and needs no hardware. Apprenticeship programs, contractors running safety training, and insurers who care about jobsite incidents would pay for it.

## 30-second version (if a judge asks "what is it?")

"It's a safety-judgment trainer for the trades. The AI plays a rookie apprentice who talks through a job and makes mistakes. You're the supervisor, and you hit STOP and say what's wrong. It grades your explanation and shows you what happens if you miss one. A new trade is just a new prompt."

## Likely judge questions

- **"How do you know the AI's trade content is right?"** The scenario prompt requires mistakes grounded in standard practice and code, like NEC and OSHA. A tradesperson reviews the cached demo scenarios, and an instructor could approve each generated job before learners see it.
- **"Why not just a quiz?"** A quiz tells you there's a mistake to find. On a jobsite nobody tells you. This trains you to notice in real time, while the work is moving, and to explain why it matters.
- **"What's next?"** An instructor dashboard tracking which hazards each apprentice misses, scenarios aligned to certification curricula, and multiplayer crew mode.
