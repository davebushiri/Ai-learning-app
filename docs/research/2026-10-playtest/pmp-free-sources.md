### 3. Scenario-writing rules

These come from the SJT and item-writing research. HumRRO best-practice blog (medium strength). McDaniel et al. 2007, *Personnel Psychology* (strong; findings not read). Haladyna, Downing and Rodriguez 2002, *Applied Measurement in Education* 15(3) (strong). Große and Renkl 2007; Adams, McLaren, Mayer et al. 2014, *Computers in Human Behavior* 36; McLaren et al. 2015, *IJAIED*. A 2025 *Educational Psychology Review* systematic review (strong).

1. **Frame it as "should do", not "would do".** We grade against best practice, which is knowledge-based instruction. The correctAction is what a competent PM *should* do.
2. **Plant one action per mistake.** HumRRO advises one action per response option. Each error step should contain a single wrong move, not a bundle, so the learner can name it precisely.
3. **Wrong moves must be plausible.** Base them on typical real errors (Haladyna): the instinctive shortcut, escalating too early, the heroic fix, skipping change control. They should not be absurd.
4. **"Best answer", not merely "correct".** The planted mistake may be *partly* reasonable but *premature or out of order*, for example escalating before assessing. Its `why` must name the better move and explain why it comes first. The state, the timing and the authority level must all be explicit in the narration.
5. **Include passive errors as well as active ones.** HumRRO distinguishes active from passive ineffective responses. Doing nothing, waiting, or ignoring a signal also counts as a mistake.
6. **Make the context decide the answer.** State the approach (predictive, agile or hybrid), the baselines, the roles and the governance in early lines, so the correct move follows from the context and not from trivia. This also covers the ECO's 40/60 mix.
7. **Contrast and explain.** Erroneous examples help most when the learner self-explains and then sees the correct version. Always pair the mistake with `correctAction` and a concrete `consequence`. Evidence is mixed and the effect is small, so don't oversell it.
8. **Originality and IP checks.** Each scenario cites an ECO task ID plus a free source for its correct action. It contains no PMBOK or Lexicon text, enabler text, or recalled exam stems. Run a similarity check against brain-dump sites.

- The current Code of Ethics text (Nov 2025 version).
- That a 2026 PMP Handbook PDF exists.
- The Kanban Guide licence.
- The PM4ID licence conflict (CC BY-NC-SA on most copies, CC BY on one).
- The Wikibooks licence version.
- The current status of NPR 7120.5F after its listed expiry.
- GAO's reproduction notice and the EVMSIG distribution marking.
- Whether the 12 Agile principles alone fall under the Manifesto's "entirety" condition.
