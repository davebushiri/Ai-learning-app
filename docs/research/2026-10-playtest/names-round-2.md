Adjacent competitors spotted:

## 3. Shortlist

1. **Hold Up!**
   - Pitch: the name is what the learner says, so the brand doubles as the game action.
   - Tagline: "Stop the Apprentice."
   - Top risk: "hold up" is everyday speech, which means false stop triggers in voice mode and a weak trademark. A logo plus the exclamation mark helps, and wake-word detection should require it at the start of an utterance.
2. **Call It**
   - Pitch: you call the mistake, and you call why.
   - Tagline: "Hear it. Call it. Own it."
   - Top risk: the "Called It" and "Calledit" prediction apps, plus the sports/betting meaning.
3. **Second Chair**
   - Pitch: the AI is your second chair, and you're the lead who catches what it gets wrong. It works for a site lead, a hiring panel or a design review.
   - Tagline: "Your apprentice talks. You take the lead."
   - Top risk: two words, and it's not a stop command. The legal and orchestra meanings may confuse some people.
4. **Flawspot**
   - Pitch: an ownable invented word that says exactly what you do.
   - Tagline: "Spot the flaw. Say why."
   - Top risk: heard aloud it can become "floss-pot" or "flaw spot". Run the radio test, and register the spaced spelling as well.
5. **Stopwise**
   - Pitch: stopping at the right moment is the skill, and "-wise" suggests mastery.
   - Tagline: "Know when to stop the apprentice."
   - Top risk: it sounds like a habit or productivity app, and "Catchwise" and "Spotwise" are already taken in other categories.

## 4. Naming best practice

- **Radio test.** Say the name once to someone who hasn't seen it and ask them to type it. Namecheap calls this the check that eliminates the most candidates. Avoid dropped vowels, numbers and dashes, and test with background noise, because consonant clusters collapse. https://www.namecheap.com/blog/domain-name-radio-test/
- **"Ask Siri" test.** For voice-first products, check that a voice assistant transcribes the name correctly. https://domaininvesting.com/does-it-pass-the-radio-test-ask-siri/
- **Verb-able names.** "Hold Up" and "Call It" already are the action, and invented words can become verbs ("I Flawspotted it").
- **Generic words are hard to own.** Descriptive or generic terms get weak or no trademark protection, while invented or arbitrary names get the strongest. https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks
- **Recommended pattern.** Pair a common-phrase name with a distinctive logo and descriptor (e.g. "Hold Up! — Stop the Apprentice"), or use an invented word as the brand and "Hold up!" as the in-game command.
- **Validate before choosing.** Test 2–4 finalists on strangers, including recall after a delay.
