**Patterns:** The literal aviation and film words are already taken by narrow tools, often for pilots. Short invented names (Tolo, Kepo) collide least but say nothing about the product, so they need a tagline. The "teaching moment" direction gave the most ownable idea, **Pennydrop**.

## 3. Combined top 8 (all rounds)

The pattern throughout: the brand is ownable, and the in-game command is a separate everyday phrase.

1. **Pennydrop**
   - Command: "Hold up!"
   - Tagline: "Stop the Apprentice. Feel the penny drop."
   - Why: it names the moment of understanding, works for any subject, and no collision has been seen (not yet checked).
   - Risk: some US listeners may not know the idiom.
2. **Flawspot** (round 2)
   - Command: "Hold up!"
   - Tagline: "Spot the flaw. Say why."
   - Why: invented and self-explaining.
   - Risk: it may be heard as "floss-pot".
3. **Second Chair** (round 2)
   - Command: "Stop!"
   - Tagline: "Your apprentice talks. You take the lead."
   - Risk: two words, and it means something specific in law and orchestras.
4. **Checkride**
   - Command: "Hold up!"
   - Tagline: "Every session is a checkride."
   - Why: an exam-prep feel without saying PMP.
   - Risk: the aviation meaning is niche, and collisions are unchecked.
5. **Errata**
   - Command: "Stop!"
   - Tagline: "Find the errata. Fix the thinking."
   - Risk: may be heard as "erada", and it's a plural noun.
6. **Tolo**
   - Command: "Hold up!"
   - Tagline: "Stop the Apprentice."
   - Why: the cleanest invented name, and it suits a .app domain.
   - Risk: the name carries no meaning by itself.
7. **Go-Around**
   - Command: "Hold up!"
   - Tagline: "Catch it. Call it. Go around."
   - Risk: the "Going Around" pilot app, and the hyphen.
8. **Stopwise** (round 2)
   - Command: "Hold up!"
   - Tagline: "Know when to stop the apprentice."
   - Risk: it sounds like a habit-tracking app.

"Hold Up!" and "Call It" stay strongest as **commands**, not brands, because of false triggers and weak trademark protection.

## 4. Five-minute radio test (one friend at a time, 5 friends)

**Setup.** Pick 3 finalists, for example Pennydrop, Flawspot and Tolo. Rotate which one you say first for each friend. Do it by phone call or with background noise, such as a café or TV, to be realistic. Write down answers exactly; don't correct anyone.

**0:00, intro (15 s).** "I'm testing names for an app. I'll say each name once and won't repeat or spell it. There are no wrong answers."

**0:15, hear and spell (about 1 min).** Say each name once, naturally, inside a sentence: "It's called *Pennydrop*." After each one: "Type or write how you'd spell it." Record their spelling.

**1:15, meaning (about 1 min).** For each name: "What do you think this app does? Who is it for?" Record their first guess, word for word.

**2:15, explain the product (30 s).** "It's a voice game. An AI apprentice does a task out loud and makes mistakes. You say 'Hold up!' and explain what's wrong. It starts with project-management exam practice, then interview prep." Then ask: "Which name fits best now, and why?"

**2:45, distractor (10 minutes, no names mentioned).** Chat about anything else, or have them do something unrelated.

**About 13:00, recall (1 min).** "What were the app names I mentioned?" This is unprompted. Then: "Which one would you search for in the App Store tomorrow?"

**Bonus voice check (30 s).** Have them say each name to Siri or Google Assistant in a search query, and record what it transcribes.

**Scoring per name:**
- Spelling: correct spellings out of 5. To pass, at least 4 of 5 spell it exactly.
- Meaning: guesses that mention learning, mistakes or practice.
- Recall: unprompted recalls out of 5. To pass, at least 3 of 5.
- Voice assistant: correct transcriptions out of 5.

Drop any name that fails spelling or recall. Bring the survivors to the public pilot.
